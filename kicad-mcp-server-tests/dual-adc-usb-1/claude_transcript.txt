  Method: I generated the netlist with mcp__kicad__generate_netlist and went
  through it pin by pin with get_netlist_components. For the ADA4817, LTC2290,
  LM27762, TPS62160 and iCE40 I also pulled the pin names from the KiCad

  Mismatches

  1. The input capacitance is about 2 pF, not the ~12 pF the note claims (AFE_A
     and AFE_B).
     - The input divider's capacitor is 2.2 pF (C101), in series with C102 +
       C103. Balancing the divider needs C103 ≈ 15 pF, so that series
       combination is 2.2 pF against 42 pF, which comes to about 2.1 pF. Board
       traces and the BNC add a few pF.
     - The divider itself is wired correctly. The trim range (31.5–47 pF) covers
       the 42 pF it needs.
     - Why it matters: the note says the input works with scope probes. 10x
       probes expect a scope input of roughly 10–25 pF, and a ~2–5 pF input may
       be outside their adjustment range. That part is my inference, not
       something I verified.
  2. The iCE40's PLL ground pins are tied straight to board ground (U601 pins 53
     and 127).
     - The note says the PLL filtering follows Lattice's iCE40 hardware
       checklist. As I remember that checklist, those pins should connect only
       through the filter capacitors and not to board ground.
     - I'm about 75% sure of this. Check Lattice's iCE40 hardware checklist
       (TN1252) before changing anything.

  Still unresolved

  - The LM27762 negative-feedback divider (R407/R408): the existing TODO in
    power.kicad_sch still stands. The wiring follows the same layout as the
    positive side, which gives about −3.97 V if the negative feedback pin
    regulates to −1.2 V. I can't confirm that without the datasheet.
  - ADA4817 feedback pin: the buffer's feedback resistor (R103, 200 Ω) goes to
    the dedicated feedback pin (pin 1), and nothing ties that pin to the output
    pin (pin 6). That's correct only if the chip connects the two internally. I
    believe it does, and 200 Ω is the value Analog Devices recommends for a
    unity-gain buffer, but I haven't confirmed it.

  Minor concern (not a note mismatch)

  - The ADC's 3.0 V supply stays off until the PC recognizes the board, but the
    FPGA is powered from the start. If the FPGA's internal pull-ups are on for
    the ADC data pins, they can leak current into the unpowered ADC. That's
    harmless at these levels, but it's simple to disable those pull-ups in the
    FPGA's pin constraints.

● The design has no obvious fatal wiring errors. The serious risks are clocking,
  analog noise from the board layout, and specs that were never verified
  against datasheets. ERC is clean. DRC shows 343 warnings and no errors, all
  cosmetic or library-mismatch. The 170 library mismatches mean the board
  footprints differ from the current KiCad library versions; that's probably
  harmless, but it's worth checking that no pads went stale.

  Could stop the board working

  1. The FPGA has no clock when it powers up. The 10 MHz oscillator runs on the
     switched 3.0 V rail, so it stays off until the PC recognizes the board. The
     FT2232H's 60 MHz clock only exists once the host puts it in sync-FIFO
     mode. The iCE40HX4K has no internal oscillator, so the FPGA configures and
     then sits with no clock. Any PLL locked to the 10 MHz clock also has to be
     reset after that clock appears.
     - Fix: power X301 from the always-on 3.3 V rail, or add a small always-on
       clock, or write the FPGA logic to expect late clocks. I'm confident about
       this one.
  2. The −4 V supply (VEE) is unverified. The LM27762 negative-feedback TODO is
     still open, and VEE powers both input buffers. If the formula is wrong, VEE
     lands at some other voltage, and the buffers either clip or run out of
     headroom. Checking the datasheet takes ten minutes.
  3. The iCE40 PLL ground pins are tied to board ground (U601 pins 53 and 127).
     If my memory of Lattice's checklist (TN1252) is right, this degrades PLL
     jitter or lock. That matters most for the 100 MHz SDRAM clock. I'm about
     75% confident.
  4. The FT2232H's EEPROM ships blank. Sync-FIFO mode only works after the
     EEPROM is programmed (e.g., with FT_PROG). This is a bring-up procedure
     step, not a hardware fault, but until it's done the board looks dead.

  Could make it miss its specs

  5. Analog noise from the PCB layout:
     - The LM27762 charge pump (U404, at 85,92) sits between the two input
       channels and about 30 mm from the ADC.
     - The USB connector (J501) and FT2232H sit right next to channel A's input
       circuitry.
     - The ADC's differential inputs drop to the bottom layer through vias. The
       plane next to the bottom layer is the +3.3 V digital supply (In2), not
       ground.
     - The divider node /AFE_A/DIV is a ~50 kΩ node with a 39 mm trace. It will
       pick up digital noise, and the trace capacitance shifts the trimmer
       setting.
     - Expect the noise floor to be noticeably worse than the ADC alone would
       give.
  6. Scope-probe compatibility: the input capacitance is about 2 pF plus stray,
     not about 12 pF. 10x probes expect a scope input of roughly 10–25 pF, so
     they may not compensate. Matching a typical probe needs a larger C101 and a
     matching change to C102/C103.
  7. USB High-Speed signal quality:
     - D+ and D− change width along their length (0.2–0.28 mm), so the impedance
       isn't controlled.
     - D+ has a via and D− doesn't.
     - The two traces differ in length by 2.5 mm.
     - The runs are short (about 20 mm), so this probably works. If it doesn't,
       the board falls back to 12 Mb/s Full-Speed. Low to medium risk.
  8. SDRAM at 100 MHz is ambitious for an iCE40 HX. Trace lengths range from 15
     to 62 mm, and the SDRAM clock comes from an ordinary I/O pin. There's a lot
     of bandwidth margin, though: running the SDRAM at 50–66 MHz still beats
     the 40 MB/s requirement by 2–3 times. So this is a risk to timing closure,
     not to function.
  9. Streaming throughput: two channels × 10 MSPS × 2 bytes is 40 MB/s, and the
     FT2232H manages about 35–40 MB/s. Capture to SDRAM then upload works fine.
     Continuous streaming would overrun. Packing samples into 3 bytes per pair
     gets it to 30 MB/s.

  Could damage the board or work only intermittently

  10. The ADC inputs can be overdriven. The THS4521 runs from 0 to +4 V, but the
      ADC's absolute maximum input is about 3.3 V (its 3.0 V supply plus 0.3
      V). An input beyond roughly ±38 V would drive an amplifier output past
      that limit, and the input clamps don't engage until about ±80 V. The 49.9
      Ω series resistors limit the current, so this is probably survivable but
      out of spec.
  11. Power-up sequencing: the ±4 V and 3.0 V supplies share one enable, but
      nothing guarantees which comes up first. The amplifier could drive the ADC
      inputs before the ADC is powered. This is the same exposure as item 10,
      only brief.
  12. USB power budget: the ~370 mA total and the under-100 mA draw before the
      PC recognizes the board are both estimates. Before recognition, the FPGA
      is configuring and the FT2232H and SDRAM are idle on 3.3 V. That's
      plausible but unmeasured. Measure it on the first board.
  13. Back-powering the ADC: the iCE40's default pull-ups can leak current into
      the unpowered ADC's data pins. Disable them in the FPGA's pin constraints.

  The cheap fixes to make before fabrication are items 1–3 and 6. Item 5 is the
  expensive one, since it means re-placing parts. It's worth doing if analog
  noise performance matters for your use.

  Sources: the netlist and DRC from the kicad MCP tools, trace lengths and
  placement pulled from dual_adc_usb.kicad_pcb, and pinouts and limits from my
  memory of the datasheets. I didn't check this time against the actual LM27762,
  ADA4817, iCE40 TN1252 or LTC2290 documents, so items 2, 3 and 10 are the ones
  to verify first.
