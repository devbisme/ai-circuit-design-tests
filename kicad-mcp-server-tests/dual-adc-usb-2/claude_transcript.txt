  Konnect's parts. Three of them (LTC2290, iCE40HX4K, FT2232H) are also what I
  picked, largely because they were the suitable ones in the KiCad library.

  Architecture (every decision's options and my pick are in DESIGN.md):
  - Inputs: BNC connectors, 1 MΩ ∥ ~19 pF so 10× scope probes can be
    compensated.
  - Front end: a ÷10 divider with a trimmer, diode clamp, OPA810 buffer and
    THS4521 differential driver. ±10 V at the BNC maps to the ADC's 2 Vpp input.
  - ADC: LTC2290, dual 12-bit at 10 MS/s, sampling both channels at the same
    instant. A separate low-jitter 10 MHz oscillator clocks it.
  - Capture: iCE40HX4K FPGA writing to a 32 MB SDRAM. 0.1 s of both channels
    needs 4 MB, so 32 MB holds 0.8 s.
  - USB: FT2232H. One channel moves samples to the host (~35–40 MB/s); the other
    programs the FPGA's flash.
  - Power: estimated about 300 mA at 5 V, so USB 2.0's 500 mA is enough. I still
    used a USB-C connector wired as a plain USB 2.0 device, for robustness.

  Verification:
  - Schematic: no ERC errors. The only warnings come from your stale global
    symbol-library table, not the design. KiCad's netlist matches my net list
    exactly (191 of 191 nets).
  - PCB: 150 × 90 mm, 4 layers, 0 unconnected, and the board matches the
    schematic.
  - DRC: 4 errors, all hole clearance (0.194 mm) inside KiCad's stock USB-C
    footprint. Check them against your fab's limit. The rest are silkscreen
    warnings.
  - Bug caught: that stock footprint numbers its shell pads SH while the symbol
    uses S1, so the shield had silently no net. I fixed it in the build script.

  Unchecked: pinouts and parameters came from my memory of the datasheets, not
  the datasheets themselves. Check these first:
  - The LM27761 feedback formula that sets the −3.3 V rail.
  - The LTC2290 reference capacitors and its MODE pin setting.
  - How the iCE40's PLL ground pins should be connected.
  - That FT2232H channel B can program the flash while channel A is configured
    for FIFO mode.
  - The pin-4/pin-5 output assignment on the THS4521: the library symbol doesn't
    name them.

  Layout weaknesses:
  - USB D+/D− are plain tracks, not a 90 Ω pair.
  - Anti-aliasing is only two poles, so signals above 5 MHz will alias.
  - About 5 connections were finished by a simple router I wrote. They have
    staircase jogs and are only 0.15 mm wide. That includes the buck regulator's
    switch node, which needs reworking as a short, wide trace.
  - Analog and digital share one ground plane.
  - Silkscreen wasn't cleaned up.

  How kicad-mcp-server held up as a design tool: its own code placed every
  symbol, made every connection and drew the board outline; I called those
  functions in bulk from a script rather than through 1,000+ separate MCP calls.
  Its gaps:
  - Library parts defined as variants of another part come in with no pins.
  - Connection labels attach to the wrong place on parts split into several
    sections (the FPGA has five).
  - Labels point sideways on tall chips.
  - It has no tools for schematic sheets, no-connect markers, netlist import,
    placement or routing.

  I filled those gaps with KiCad's Python API and Freerouting. Freerouting only
  got close once I shrank vias to 0.45 mm and tracks to 0.15 mm; it then left
  two real unrouted connections. DESIGN.md lists the rebuild steps. The last few
  repairs were done by hand and aren't fully scripted.
