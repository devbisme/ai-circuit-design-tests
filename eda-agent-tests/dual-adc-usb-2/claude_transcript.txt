● I built the dual-ADC board described in dual-adc-prompt.txt: a connected
  schematic, a routed 4-layer PCB, and fab outputs. DRC shows 0 errors and ERC 0
  violations. The FT232H and LTC2290 support circuits come from memory, so
  check them before ordering boards.

  eda-agent alone didn't get there. Its generated schematic has no connections
  at all, so scripts in scripts/ filled the gaps.

  Design choices (options and reasons are in design_decisions.md)
  - Inputs: two BNC connectors at 1 MΩ, each through a compensated ÷11 divider,
    an OPA1656 buffer and a THS4521 differential driver.
  - ADC: LTC2290, a dual 12-bit 10 MS/s part.
  - Capture: the full rate is 40 MB/s, which is at the limit of what USB 2.0 can
    stream. So an iCE40HX4K FPGA records into 32 MB of SDRAM (0.8 s at full
    rate, against the 0.1 s required) and then uploads.
  - USB: FT232H at USB 2.0 high speed, with a USB-B connector. The estimated
    draw is ~330 mA of the 500 mA allowed, so USB-C wasn't needed.

  Checks
  - Schematic: 7 sheets, ERC 0 violations, and the exported netlist matches the
    plan net for net.
  - PCB: 125 × 95 mm, 4 layers. DRC shows 0 errors, 0 unconnected items and 0
    schematic-mismatch issues; 19 silkscreen warnings remain.
  - eda-agent's full review: 0 errors. Its warnings are the intentional
    no-connect pins and the mounting/paste pads that have no net.
  - Outputs: Gerbers, drill and position files are in kicad/fab/, with the BOM,
    schematic PDF and a 3D render in kicad/.

  How eda-agent did
  - Useful: its design-plan validator caught real mistakes, its generated board
    had every pad on the right net (725 of 725), and its review, Gerber, drill
    and 3D-render tools worked.
  - Schematic: unusable. Every net label sits on a grid and touches no pin (780
    ERC violations), and multi-part chips like the FPGA get only their first
    section.
  - Libraries: finding real symbols needs KiCad running, and it can't read KiCad
    10's symbol libraries; it used the system KiCad 9 ones.
  - Layout: it has no placement, routing or copper-pour tools for KiCad. I
    scripted placement, used Freerouting (4 runs), and wrote a small router plus
    some manual fixes for the last few connections around the ±4 V supply chip.
  - Connection quirk: a leftover socket file from a closed KiCad blocked its
    connection to KiCad until I relinked the socket.

  Before fabrication
  - Unverified circuits: both datasheet downloads failed (FTDI returned 403,
