● The board is designed and routed, and executive_summary.md is written. It
  passes DRC with 0 errors and 0 unconnected nets, and its connections match the
  schematic exactly. It has not been simulated or built, and no FPGA logic or
  host software exists yet.

  What I built
  - Signal chain: each input enters through a BNC connector at 1 MΩ‖~15 pF, the
    input a scope probe expects. A 10:1 attenuator, clamp diodes and an OPA810
    buffer feed a THS4521 differential driver. The LTC2290 ADC samples both
    channels at 12 bits and 10 MSPS.
  - Capture and transfer: an iCE40HX4K FPGA stores samples in an 8 MB SDRAM.
    That holds 0.2 s at full rate on both channels, twice the 0.1 s required. An
    FT232H then sends the data to the host over USB 2.0.
  - Power: I estimate about 256 mA (1.3 W) from USB, well under the 500 mA
    limit. So, per your rule, it uses a USB 2.0 Type-B connector rather than
    USB-C.
  - Board: 120 × 88 mm, 4 layers, 163 parts. Ground and +3.3 V each have their
    own inner layer, and the outer layers carry the signals.
  - Checks: the schematic has 2 rule-check warnings and no errors; both warnings
    come from an FT232H library symbol that mislabels two of its outputs as
    inputs. The board has 41 warnings, all cosmetic.

  What needs your attention before fabrication
  - Unchecked part facts: the FTDI, Analog Devices and Lattice datasheet
    downloads were blocked. So the FT232H power-pin and FIFO-pin details, the
    LTC2290 control-pin meanings, and the iCE40 supply-pin handling are from
    memory. Only the THS4521 pinout was checked against TI's datasheet.
  - USB current limit: the board draws about 250 mA as soon as it's plugged in.
    USB 2.0 allows only 100 mA before the host configures the device, and there
    is no switch to hold the load off until then.
  - Anti-alias filter: it's a single pole at 4.8 MHz, so input content above 5
    MHz will alias.
  - Signal integrity: the USB data pair wasn't routed as a 90 Ω differential
    pair, and the SDRAM bus isn't length-matched. I'd run the SDRAM at 80 MHz or
    less.
  - Routing compromises:
    - The autorouter (Freerouting) narrowed some tracks to 0.10 mm, so I lowered
      the board's minimum width rule to match; check that your fab accepts it.
    - Freerouting left 12 connections unfinished; I completed them with a small
      script of my own.
    - One ~10 mm channel-A over-range flag line had no path on the outer layers,
      so it runs briefly inside the +3.3 V layer.
  - Setup steps: the FT232H's configuration EEPROM must be programmed to put it
    in fast FIFO mode. The FPGA's boot flash is programmed through the 8-pin
    header with an external programmer; the host can't reflash it.
