● The dual-channel ADC board is designed and routed, and the fab outputs are
  written. Before ordering, I'd fix one ADC power-up problem I left open (item 1
  below).

  What it is: 100 × 80 mm, 4 layers, 239 parts. The signal chain is BNC inputs →
  analog front end → ADS5231 dual ADC → Spartan-6 FPGA with a 16 MB SDRAM →
  FT232H → USB-C carrying USB 2.0.

  Checks:
  - Schematic: 0 ERC errors, and the KiCad netlist matches the source exactly
    (217/217 nets).
  - Review: no hard failures, and the BOM matches the code.
  - Board: fully routed, 0 DRC errors, release gate PASS.

  Key decisions (the reasoning is in architecture/):
  - Capture, then upload: both channels at full rate need 40 MB/s, which is at
    the practical USB 2.0 limit. The board records into the SDRAM, which holds
    0.42 s (0.1 s required), then uploads.
  - Anti-alias filter: −80.9 dB at 5 MHz in simulation, −78.7 dB worst case; 12
    bits needs 72 dB. The official check shows "not checked" because boardforge
    can't read the custom op-amp's pin names. The reviewer re-ran the same check
    with the names mapped, and it passed.
  - Power: worst case 0.43 A, so a standard USB 2.0 port is enough. I used a
    USB-C connector anyway.

  What needs your attention:
  1. ADC power-up: for a moment at power-up, the ADS5231's limit between its two
     supply pins (±0.3 V) may be exceeded. The fix is one connection (U701's
     enable to +3V3), but it changes a block's interface, which means another
     architect, coder and review pass. I deferred it.
  2. Bug in boardforge's fab output: the parts list groups designators into
     ranges like C1001-C1022, and the placement-file step doesn't expand them.
     It silently dropped 80 of 239 parts. I regenerated both files in build/fab/
     with all 239 parts. Re-running boardforge fab will bring the bug back. The
     fix belongs in physical.py (pass --ref-range-delimiter "" to the KiCad BOM
     export) or in _expand_designators.
  3. Sourcing risk: the ADS5231 has one source and only 152 in stock.
  4. Unverified parts:
     - The 12 MHz crystal has no datasheet, so its pinout is assumed.
     - The oscillator's jitter is unspecified.
     - The SDRAM facts come from a sibling part's datasheet.
  5. Custom footprints: check the BNC and trimmer footprints against their
     drawings.
  6. Not included: FPGA gateware, the FT232H EEPROM setup and host software.

  The run cost about $23 at API rates. I didn't commit anything to git. Details
  are in executive_summary.md.
