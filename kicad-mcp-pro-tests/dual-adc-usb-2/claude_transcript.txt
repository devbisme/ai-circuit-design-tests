● The design is finished: a 4-layer, 115 × 85 mm board with Gerbers. It passes
  DRC (0 errors, 0 unconnected, matches the schematic) and ERC (0 errors). It
  has not been simulated or built, and three part values still need checking

  Design choices (options and reasoning are in design_notes.md)
  - Inputs: two BNC connectors so standard scope probes fit. Each has a 1 MΩ ÷10
    input divider (±10 V becomes ±1 V), protection diodes, an OPA810 buffer,
    and a THS4521 driving the ADC.
  - Converter: LTC2290, a dual 12-bit 10 MS/s ADC, so it matches the spec
    exactly. It runs from its own 10 MHz oscillator, because an FPGA-generated
    clock is too jittery for 12 bits.
  - Capture: an iCE40HX4K FPGA with 8 MB of SDRAM. That holds 0.2 s of samples
    on both channels at full rate, twice what you asked for.
  - USB: an FT2232HL. One channel moves the data: a 4 MB capture uploads in
    about 0.5 s at 8 MB/s. The other channel lets you reprogram the FPGA's flash
    over USB.
  - Power: my estimate is about 1.6 W, from typical datasheet figures, not
    measured. USB 2.0 supplies 2.5 W, so I used a USB-B connector rather than
    USB-C. The board draws more than 100 mA before USB enumeration; that's
    common for FTDI-based boards but not strictly compliant.

  Open items
  - Check the feedback resistors for the ±4.5 V supply chip (LM27762) against
    its datasheet; I wasn't sure of its equations.
  - Check two other values I wasn't certain of: the LTC2290 reference decoupling
    and the FPGA's VPP pin tied to 3.3 V.
  - The input-divider capacitors may need a trimmer to compensate properly.
  - Freerouting thinned 23 track segments to 0.112 mm. That's fine for typical
    4-layer fab rules but tighter than 5 mil.
  - 18 silkscreen warnings remain, mostly where the BNC and USB connectors
    overhang the board edge. I hid the reference designators on small parts

  - There's no FPGA code, no circuit simulation, and no part numbers in the BOM.
