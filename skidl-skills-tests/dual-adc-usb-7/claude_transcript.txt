  The decision that shaped everything: 2 ch × 10 MSPS × 2 B = 40 MB/s, which USB
  2.0 HS cannot carry reliably. So it's burst-to-memory then read out, not
  streaming. That made "≥4 MB at 40 MB/s write" the keystone, and the in-package
  PSRAM is what let it be solved without an external memory chip and its pin
  cost.

  USB-C: your conditional triggered, then partly un-triggered — the final budget
  (424 mA worst) does fit a 500 mA legacy port. I kept Type-C anyway: 85%
  loading of a legacy port is thin, and a Type-C jack with 5.1 kΩ CC pulldowns
  works on an A-to-C cable regardless, so it's a strict superset.

  Defects caught that ERC cannot see

  - JTAG header on the wrong rail — J3 referenced to 3.3 V while the JTAG pins
    are BANK3 at 1.8 V (abs max 2.1 V). Would have overdriven by 1.2 V and
    back-powered the PSRAM bank on pod insertion.
  - 1.8 V PSRAM rail absent entirely from the original design — the sample
    buffer had no supply.
  - VCORE/VCCA on the FT232H tied to 3.3 V, back-driving an internal LDO.
  - Input clamp specified to one rail — not a clamp.
  - Five separate decoupling shortfalls, including an op amp with no bypass at
    all.
  - A soft-start fix that didn't work — an RC on a binary logic enable delays
    turn-on without changing dV/dt.

  Before you lay this out

  1. J1/J2 and J4 sit on other manufacturers' land patterns.
     validate-footprints.py only checks that a library file exists. Drawings are
     on disk. Must fix.
  2. Bench-verify VCCIO3 rises ≥180 µs monotonic, with the JTAG pod attached —
     U15's ON tied to its own VIN is outside TI's characterized condition.
     Fallback: RC on U15.ON, 2 parts.
  3. Single-source risk: ADS5231 (154 pcs) and GW1NR-9 (173 pcs) are each the
     entire JLCPCB float. Fine for 5 boards, not for volume. Buy in one order.
  4. Cost ≈$88.9–90.9/board at qty 5 — top of range ~$0.9 over the $90 target I
     invented, not a number you gave me.

  Where I'd push back on my own spec: I set SNR ≥60 dB (≈10 ENOB), not true
  12-bit accuracy, because you specified resolution. That's the largest cost
  lever in the design. If you want 12 noise-free bits, that requirement — not
  the ADC — is what to revisit first.
