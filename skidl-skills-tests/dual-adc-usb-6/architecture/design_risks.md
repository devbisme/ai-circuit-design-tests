# Design risks (dual_adc_usb)

Ordered by what can actually kill the board. "Owner" is the phase that must close it.

## R1 — Sample-clock coupling aliases straight to DC (EMI, severity: high)

The ADC clock is 10.000 MHz and the sample rate is 10.000 MSa/s, so **any 10 MHz clock
energy that leaks into the analog path aliases to exactly 0 Hz** and appears as a DC offset
that drifts with temperature and layout — the one spur the anti-alias filter cannot remove
(the filter's −28 dB at 10 MHz applies to what enters the BNC, not to what couples in after
it). Mitigation, all for the layout phase: keep X1 and `CLK_ADC` on the digital side of the
partition, never route them under the front-end amps or the BNC, guard the clock trace with
ground on both sides, and series-terminate at the source (33 Ω) to slow the edge.
Owner: layout.

## R2 — Oscillator phase jitter is an unverified keystone fact (severity: high)

Requirement 7 sets ≤5 ps RMS. The jitter-limited SNR budget is
t_j = 10^(−74/20)/(2π·4.45 MHz) = **7.1 ps** for 12 bits at the top of the passband.
**No 10 MHz oscillator in the JLCPCB catalogue publishes an RMS phase-jitter figure.** The
chosen SX3M10.000B10F20TNN is a conventional 3.3 V CMOS XO, a class that typically measures
1–5 ps RMS (12 kHz–20 MHz), but that is an assumption, not a datasheet line.
Owner: phase 4 (datasheet librarian) — find the number, and if it is worse than 5 ps or
absent, substitute a SiTime SiT8008/SiT8021-class MEMS oscillator and re-check stock.

## R3 — ADC stock is thin and single-sourced (severity: high)

AD9237BCPZ-40: **211 pieces**, and the board uses two, so the catalogue holds ~105 boards.
Below the 500-piece "warn" threshold. The vetted second source AD9235BCPZ-40 has only 139.
Neither is Basic/Preferred. Owner: phase 3 — verify live stock, buy for the whole build in
one go, and confirm the AD9235 pinout is a genuine drop-in before relying on it.

## R4 — Four parts need custom KiCad symbols; one needs a custom footprint (severity: medium)

`find-symbol.py` reports MISSING for **AD9237BCPZ-40, AD9235BCPZ-40, XC6SLX9-2TQG144C,
W9825G6KH-6I, TPH2501-TR, SY8089AAC, TPS22919DCKR**. The BNC jack (KH-BNC50-3511) needs a
custom footprint as well. Found: `Amplifier_Difference:THS4521ID`,
`MCU_Cypress:CY7C68013A-56LTX` (PREFIX — confirm the QFN-56 variant),
`Regulator_Linear:TPS73633DBV`, `Memory_Flash:W25Q32JVSS`, `Power_Protection:USBLC6-2SC6`,
`74xGxx:74LVC1G17`, `Diode:BAV19x`. A 144-pin FPGA symbol generated by hand is the single
biggest transcription-error risk in the build. Owner: phase 4, via `pdf-table.py` -> `kipart`.

## R5 — Power integrity: switcher ripple into a 12-bit front end (severity: medium)

Both bucks run at 1 MHz, inside the 4.45 MHz passband. Every analog rail is therefore behind
a TPS73633 LDO (30 µVrms, 58 dB PSRR at 10 kHz) fed from `VBUS_SW`, **not** from a buck
output — this is requirement P6/decision 13 and it must not be "optimised" away in layout.
PSRR at 1 MHz is much worse than at 10 kHz, so add a ferrite + 10 µF in front of each LDO.
One LSB at the ADC is 2 V/4096 = **488 µV**; 30 µVrms of LDO noise is 0.06 LSB, and the
budget for switcher feedthrough is what remains. Owner: layout + phase 5 decoupling.

**R5 addendum (rev.2) — the ferrite allocation is four parts, not two.** R5 as written named
only the input-side pair. The `power` block placed FB1/FB2 on the LDO *inputs* (R5's literal
prescription) and FB3/FB4 on the LDO *outputs* as a second isolation stage, and recorded that
as an assumption. **Blessed as built** — `net_plan.md` now carries the full allocation table.
Arithmetic behind the blessing (BLM21PG601SN1D, R_DC = **140 mΩ**, C41556732, live-verified):

- **Cost:** 55 mA × 0.140 Ω = **7.7 mV** on `+3V3A_ADC`; 43 mA × 0.140 Ω = **6.0 mV** on
  `+3V3A_AMP`. Both are outside the LDO loop (TPS73633 senses at its OUT pin) and both are
  far inside the supply tolerance of every part they feed.
- **Benefit:** at 1 MHz — where the bucks switch and where the TPS73633's 58 dB PSRR has
  already collapsed — the bead is ≈40 Ω into C16‖C26 = 1.1 µF (|Z| = 0.145 Ω), i.e.
  **≈49 dB** of rejection that nothing else in the chain provides. FB3/FB4 are doing R5's
  job at the one frequency R5 cares about. **Do not delete them either.**
- **The residual they create, stated rather than implied:** C12 (1 µF) – FB3 – C16‖C26
  (1.1 µF) is a series-resonant tank. With the bead's low-frequency L ≈ 1 µH and
  C_eff = 1·1.1/2.1 = 0.524 µF, `f0 = 1/(2π·√(1 µH · 0.524 µF))` = **≈220 kHz**, damped only
  by 0.14 Ω of DCR plus MLCC ESR → **Q ≈ 9**, a ~19 dB peak. Excited by the LDO's own noise
  floor (~30 nV/√Hz there), that contributes ≈ 30 nV/√Hz × 9 × √(220 kHz/9 × π/2) ≈ **50 µVrms**
  — **0.10 LSB** against the 488 µV LSB, before the amps' and the ADC's own PSRR. Accepted
  for this 10-board run. **The bead's low-frequency inductance is an estimate, not a
  datasheet number** — if bench measurement shows rail noise near 200 kHz, the fix is to make
  C16/C17 a 10 µF X7R (its ESR damps the tank) or add a 1 Ω/1 µF damping leg; neither needs a
  layout change. Owner: bench bring-up.

## R6 — Single ground plane, partitioned placement (severity: medium)

Y5 (SOFT) forbids a split plane with a stitch, which is correct here. The discipline instead
is placement: BNCs and the front end at one end, FPGA/SDRAM/FX2 at the other, the ADCs on
the boundary with their AGND pins facing the analog side, and **no digital return current
routed under the analog section**. The SDRAM bus (38 lines at 100 MHz) is the loudest thing
on the board. Owner: layout.

## R7 — Gain and offset accuracy before calibration (severity: low, spec-visible)

Chain gain = (1/11) x 1.1 = 0.1000 nominal, built from 1 % resistors in the divider and the
FDA network -> worst-case ±2 % (RSS ≈ ±1 %), at the edge of F12's ±2 %. Offset comes from
`VREF_OFF` = 1.8068 V ±1 % -> ±16 mV at the attenuator node = **±1.6 % FS**, above F12's
±1 %. Both are SOFT and both are explicitly "before host-side calibration". Tightening
either means 0.1 % resistors. Owner: accepted; record in the user-facing notes.

## R8 — FX2 slave-FIFO timing and FPGA toolchain (severity: medium, firmware)

The classic FX2 pitfall is the FLAGB/FLAGC-to-SLWR latency: flags are delayed relative to
IFCLK, so the FPGA must treat "almost full" as the stop condition, not "full". 8-bit at
48 MHz gives 48 MB/s against a ~43 MB/s USB HS ceiling — 11 % margin, adequate because the
drain is not real-time. Separately, the XC6SLX9 is supported only by ISE 14.7, a
discontinued toolchain that still runs but receives no fixes. Owner: firmware (out of this
pipeline's scope) — flagged so nobody discovers it after fabrication.

## R9 — Thermal (severity: low)

Worst dissipator is the ADC LDO at (5.0 − 3.3) x 54.5 mA = **93 mW**; SOT-23-5 θJA ≈
200 °C/W -> 19 °C rise -> 69 °C junction at Y3's 50 °C ambient ceiling. The FPGA at ~300 mW
in an LQFP-144 rises ~12 °C. Nothing needs a heatsink or a thermal via field. Owner: closed.

## R10 — Inrush and USB legality (severity: low, closed by arithmetic)

≤10 µF on raw VBUS (the USB 2.0 limit), 47 µF downstream of TPS22919; inrush =
47 µF x 5 V / 2 ms = **118 mA**. Steady state is 293 mA of a 450 mA budget. The failure mode
to avoid is someone adding bulk capacitance *ahead* of the load switch during layout.
Owner: layout review.

## R11 — Compensation drift from stray capacitance (severity: low)

     and with the correction that R102 does NOT isolate that capacitance from the divider at

**R102 does not hide the clamp node from the divider.** At the compensation corner
(1/2π·20 µs ≈ 8 kHz) the 1.00 kΩ of R102 is negligible against the divider's 82.6 kΩ Thevenin
source, so **everything hanging on `CHn_BUFIN` appears in parallel with C101**. R102 only
starts isolating in the MHz decade. Computed from the real values:

| clamp-node C | τ_top = R100·C100 | τ_bot = R101·(C101+C_node) | mismatch | HF-vs-DC gain step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7.5 pF (BAV199 + amp + trace, no TVS) | 20.00 µs | 20.68 µs | +3.4 % | −0.265 dB (−3.0 %) |
| **8.4 pF (with ESD9L5.0ST5G, rev.2)** | 20.00 µs | **20.76 µs** | **+3.8 %** | **−0.296 dB (−3.4 %)** |
| 22.5 pF (with ESD9B5.0ST5G, rev.1 part) | 20.00 µs | 22.04 µs | **+10.2 %** | −0.772 dB (−8.5 %) |

The selected 0.9 pF part contributes **0.4 %** of that mismatch; the rest is the BAV199, the
amplifier and the trace, which this risk already owned. The rejected 15 pF part would have
tripled it and put a **−0.77 dB flat droop** across the whole passband — a second, independent
reason it could not stay. The amplifier input
(~3 pF), BAV199 (~3 pF) and PCB stray (~1.5 pF) add to the 220 pF bottom leg, a **~3–4 %**
mismatch that shows as a slow settling tail on square-wave edges with a 20 µs time constant. Requirement I2 asked
for a *trimmable* compensation cap; **no SMD trimmer capacitor was found in the JLCPCB
catalogue**, so this design uses fixed C0G parts and budgets the stray into the 220 pF value.
Overturning I2's "trimmable" wording is deliberate — see the handoff's `## Carried forward`.
Owner: accepted; layout must keep the node area small and the amp close.

## R12 — Clamp-node leakage is signal error, and the TVS's is bounded but unverified (severity: low, rev.2)

`CHn_BUFIN` is driven from a Thevenin source of 909 kΩ ‖ 90.9 kΩ = **82.6 kΩ**. Any DC current
pulled out of that node is an input-referred offset of 82.6 kΩ × I × 11:

- **1 nA → 0.9 mV RTI → 0.005 % FS.** This is why D100 is a BAV199 (~1 nA) and not a jellybean.
- **1 µA → 83 mV at the node → 0.91 V RTI → 4.5 % FS.** That is the number that would matter.

The selected ESD9L5.0ST5G specifies **Ir = 1 µA max at the full 5 V standoff**. The node
actually sits at 0.73–2.55 V, where the real leakage is orders of magnitude lower, and the
1 µA figure is a 150 °C-corner maximum. **Assumption carried:** leakage at ≤2.6 V and ≤85 °C
is ≤50 nA, i.e. ≤0.23 % FS, inside R7's already-accepted ±1.6 % FS offset error. This is the
one spec on the new part that was **not** verified against a datasheet curve — the LCSC
parametric line is the only source. Owner: phase-4 datasheet librarian should pull the onsemi
ESD9L5.0ST5G leakage-vs-voltage curve and confirm; a bench DC offset check on the first board
settles it either way. Substituting a TVS with a higher Ir spec is **not** a safe swap.

## R13 — ESD energy now lands on the attenuator mid node (severity: low, rev.2, accepted)

Moving the clamp behind R102 is correct for F2/I2/I3, but it means a fast strike on the BNC
centre pin couples through C100 (22 pF) into `CHn_ATT` before anything clamps it. Charge
divides C100 : (C101 + C_node), i.e. 22 : 228 pF, so an 8 kV IEC contact strike puts on the
order of **700 V** on the mid node for the first few nanoseconds, until R102 (220 ns into
220 pF) bleeds it into the clamp pair. C100/C101 are 100 V C0G 0603 and R100 is an 0805.

This is the conventional scope-front-end compromise and it is what the sourced parts were
already chosen for (rev.5 re-sourced C100 from 0402/50 V to 0603/100 V for exactly this
family of reasons). It is **not** made worse by the rev.2 change — the rev.1 arrangement had
the same exposure plus a TVS that failed short at rated input. Owner: accepted; if field
returns show input failures, the fix is a high-standoff (≥60 V) low-C TVS **at the BNC**, and
its capacitance then has to come out of C100's 22 pF to hold I2's 20.0 pF.
