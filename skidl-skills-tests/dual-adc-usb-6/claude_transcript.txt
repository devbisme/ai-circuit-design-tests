  The one architectural consequence of your spec worth restating: 2 ch × 10
  MSa/s × 16 bit is 40 MB/s, at or above the practical USB 2.0 ceiling. So this
  captures to RAM and drains afterward — it cannot stream continuously at full
  rate, and nothing in the design pretends otherwise.

  What the process actually caught

  Four defects that would have cost a board spin, none of which ERC can detect:

  - The input TVS sat on the BNC node, conducting 2.2 V past breakdown at the 
    board's own rated ±10 V input. It survived four phases because a BOM cell
    said "≤0.5 pF" for a 15 pF part.
  - The FX2 reset RC was 5× too short — the bridge would have released reset
    before its PLL locked.
  - Two footprints had matching pad counts and wrong packages. Both passed
    validate-footprints.py green, including when I ran it.
  - A 10 kΩ placeholder on the FPGA mode pins would have parked M1 at an invalid
    logic level.

  The recurring mechanism is worth naming: every one was caught by an agent
  re-deriving something it had been handed as settled. The architect corrected
  my pole arithmetic, a coder corrected my justification for the isolation
  resistor, the sourcer corrected the architect's capacitance model, and the
  footprint agent corrected the sourcer's reading of a drawing. The reviewer
  caught its own miss on second pass.

  Open items

  One, and it isn't about this board: validate-footprints.py resolves library
  paths and never sees the sourced MPN's package, so it structurally cannot
  catch a package-variant mismatch. That's a plugin tooling gap, logged as
  OI-21.

  Three things are accepted with the risk recorded rather than resolved — TVS
